# Lab: Explore your printer settings (Windows)

**Time:** 15 minutes. **Needs:** a Windows PC. A real printer is helpful but not required. Nothing here changes any hardware.

Goal: see the drivers, queue, languages and settings from Part 6 on a real machine.

## 1. See your printers and their drivers

Open **Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Printers & scanners**. Click any printer (even "Microsoft Print to PDF"), then **Printer properties**.

On the **Advanced** tab, note the **Driver** name. Everything the video said about drivers is shown here.

## 2. Look at the print queue

Click **Open print queue**. Print a test page to "Microsoft Print to PDF" and watch the job appear and leave. This is the *print queue* a print server manages for a whole office.

## 3. Find the settings the exam names

Open **Printing preferences** for any printer and find:

- **Orientation** (portrait or landscape)
- **Duplex** or "Print on both sides" (some printers don't have it)
- **Paper source** or tray, and paper size and type
- **Quality** (draft, normal, best)

Write down which of these your printer offers.

## 4. Printer languages

In **Printer properties > Advanced**, look at the driver name. Many drivers include "PCL" or "PS" (PostScript) in the name. Which one is yours?

## 5. Printer sharing

In **Printer properties > Sharing**, look at the option to share the printer. Don't turn it on unless you want to. Note why a printer shared this way depends on the PC being on, and what a print server does differently.

## Check yourself

- Which printer type prints multipart carbon-copy forms?
- Which maintenance step should come before cleaning inside a laser printer?
- What does secured print protect against?

## Going further (optional)

If you have a real inkjet, find its **Maintenance** or **Utility** tab and look for **Clean printheads** and **Align** or **Calibrate**. Only run these if you actually have a print quality problem, because cleaning uses ink.
