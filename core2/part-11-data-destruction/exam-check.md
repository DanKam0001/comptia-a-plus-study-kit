# Exam check: Data Destruction and Disposal

Cover the answers and try each one out loud first.

**1.** A company is selling its old office laptops. The drives held customer records, and a staff member plans to just delete the files and format the drives. What is wrong with this plan?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>A standard format leaves the data recoverable, so the drives should be wiped or destroyed. Why: deleting and a standard format only remove the index.</details>

**2.** Someone suggests using a strong magnet, called a degausser, to wipe an old solid state drive. Will this work?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>No. Degaussing only affects magnetic media, not SSD chips. Use secure erase or shred the drive.</details>

**3.** A company hires an outside firm to destroy its old drives. How can it get proof afterwards that the drives were really destroyed?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Ask for a certificate of destruction. Why: it's signed proof listing the drives, date and method. Keep it as evidence.</details>

## More practice (written for this kit)

**4.** Which destruction method is carried out by a licensed facility and burns the drive?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Incineration.</details>

**5.** A drive will be donated to a school. Which method keeps the drive usable but removes the data?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Wiping (a full overwrite, or a low-level format).</details>

**6.** A failed hard drive holding sensitive data can't be wiped. What do you do?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Physically destroy it: drill, shred, degauss (it's magnetic) or incinerate.</details>

**7.** What does a quick format actually do?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>Builds a new empty file index. The old data is still on the disk.</details>

**8.** Where should old computers and batteries go?
<details><summary>Answer</summary>An approved e-waste recycler, not the normal bin.</details>
