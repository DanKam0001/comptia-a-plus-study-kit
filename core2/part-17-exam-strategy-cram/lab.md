# Lab: Take a timed mock exam and build your weak-spot list

**Time:** 90 minutes for the mock, plus 20 minutes afterwards. **Needs:** the free Core 2 practice exam from this kit (`exams/core2`), a timer, and a pen or notes app. Nothing here touches your computer settings.

Goal: practise the exam techniques from Part 17 under real conditions.

## 1. Set up exam conditions

1. Open `exams/core2/practice-exam-1-questions` (Word or Markdown). Keep the answer key **closed**.
2. Close other tabs and silence your phone.
3. Set a timer for **90 minutes** (a real exam gives you 90 minutes for at most 90 questions).

## 2. Sit it with the techniques

- Aim for about **1 minute per question**.
- If you are stuck, write your **best guess**, put a **flag** next to the number, and move on.
- Underline the words **first, best, most likely, next** in each question.
- When the timer shows 15 minutes left, stop and go back to your flagged questions.
- **Answer every question.** Never leave a blank.

## 3. Mark it and find the weak spots

1. Open the answer key and mark your paper.
2. For every miss, write down its topic and, if the answer key shows one, its **objective number**.
3. Use the cram map in the cheat sheet to find which **part** covers it.
4. Count your misses per part. The part with the most misses is where to spend your next study session.

## 4. Practise a PBQ yourself

PBQs reward real practice. Pick three tasks and do each on a real PC in under five minutes:

- Use `ipconfig` and `ping` to check a network connection.
- Open Disk Management (`diskmgmt.msc`) and read the partition layout without changing anything.
- Open Task Manager and find the Startup tab and the Services tab.

## Check yourself

- What is the pass mark on the 100 to 900 scale? (700)
- What do you do when a PBQ looks unfamiliar? (do what you know, flag it, come back)
- Which words signal what the question is really asking? (first, best, most likely, next)
