# Lab: A safety and policy audit of your own workspace

**Time:** 15 to 20 minutes. **Needs:** your own PC or laptop and a pen. Nothing here changes any setting. Do not open a PC case unless it is switched off and unplugged.

Goal: spot the real-world versions of the things in Part 15.

## 1. Walk round your workspace

Tick each item, and write what you would change:

- Is any cable a trip hazard? (**cable management**)
- Are the vents on your PC or laptop clear, and is it away from heat? (**placement, ventilation**)
- Is there visible dust in the vents or fans? (**dust cleanup**; use compressed air or a vacuum, with the PC off)
- Is everything plugged into a **surge suppressor**? Is there a **UPS** on anything important?
- Where is your nearest fire extinguisher, and is it suitable for electrical fires?

## 2. Check your disposal plan

Find one old battery, one dead charger or one old device at home. Search for your local council's e-waste or battery recycling drop-off point. Write down where it would go. That is **proper disposal** instead of the bin.

## 3. Read a real license

Open the license or terms for one app you use (Settings > Apps, or the app's About page). Write down:

- Is it **personal-use** or **corporate-use**?
- Is it **perpetual** or a subscription?
- Is it **open source**? (Search the app name plus "license".)

## 4. Spot the policy

Next time you sign into a work, school or public Wi-Fi system, look for a **splash screen** or banner with rules. Is it an **AUP** (acceptable use policy)? What does it forbid?

## 5. Role-play a difficult customer

Ask a friend to play an angry customer ("this computer is useless!"). Practise this script out loud:

1. Stay calm and let them finish without interrupting.
2. Restate: "So the laptop shuts down when you open the browser. Is that right?"
3. Ask one open-ended question: "What was happening just before it shut down?"
4. Set expectations: "I'll check it today and update you by 4pm."

## Check yourself

- What is the very first step before opening a PC to repair it? (disconnect the power)
- Why do you work from a copy of a drive in an investigation? (to preserve the original and its integrity)
- Which device keeps a PC running through a power cut? (a UPS)
