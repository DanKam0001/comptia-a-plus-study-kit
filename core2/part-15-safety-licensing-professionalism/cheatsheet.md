## 4.4 Common safety procedures

| Topic | What to remember |
|---|---|
| **ESD straps** | Wrist band clipped to bare metal on the case, so static drains away from you instead of into a part |
| **ESD mats** | A static-safe surface to work on or stand on |
| **Antistatic bags** | Store and carry loose parts in them |
| **Electrical safety / equipment grounding** | Grounding sends stray electricity safely into the earth |
| **Proper component handling and storage** | Hold cards by the edges, don't touch contacts, store parts properly |
| **Cable management** | Stops trip hazards and keeps airflow clear |
| **Compliance with government regulations** | Follow the safety rules that apply where you work |

**Personal safety**

- **Disconnect power before repairing a PC.** Do this first.
- **Lifting techniques:** bend the knees, keep your back straight, lift with your legs, get help with heavy items.
- **Fire safety:** know where the extinguisher is, use the right type for electrical fires, never use water on one.
- **Safety goggles:** protect your eyes (cleaning, cutting, cables under tension).
- **Air filter mask:** protects your lungs from dust.

## 4.5 Environmental impacts and local environment controls

- **MSDS (material safety data sheet):** documentation for handling and disposal of a product. (Many workplaces now call it an SDS, safety data sheet. The exam objectives say MSDS.)
  - **Proper battery disposal**, **proper toner disposal**, **proper disposal of other devices and assets**: never in the normal rubbish. Use recycling or hazardous-waste collection.
- **Temperature, humidity-level awareness and proper ventilation**
  - **Location / equipment placement:** away from heat sources, with space around vents.
  - Too dry means static. Too humid means condensation and corrosion.
  - **Dust cleanup:** compressed air or a vacuum.
- **Power surges, under-voltage events and power losses**
  - **Surge suppressor:** blocks sudden spikes.
  - **Uninterruptible power supply (UPS):** battery that keeps equipment running through under-voltage events and power losses, long enough to shut down safely.

## 4.6 Prohibited content/activity, privacy, licensing and policy

### Incident response

- **Inform management and law enforcement as necessary.** Do not investigate on your own.
- **Chain of custody:** a written record of who handled the evidence and when.
- **Copy of drive:** work from a copy, so the original is untouched (data integrity and preservation).
- **Incident documentation:** write down everything you saw and did.
- **Order of volatility:** collect the most short-lived data first. Memory before disk.

### Licensing

- **Licensing / DRM / EULA:** DRM (digital rights management) stops illegal copying. The EULA (end-user license agreement) sets the rules for using the software.
- **Valid licenses** are required.
- **Perpetual license:** buy once, keep.
- **Personal-use vs corporate-use license:** a personal license can't cover a business.
- **Open-source license:** free to use and change under its own terms.

### Agreements, regulated data and policy

- **NDA / MNDA:** (mutual) non-disclosure agreement. A promise to keep information secret. A mutual NDA binds both sides.
- **Regulated data:** credit card payment information, personal government-issued information, **PII** (personally identifiable information), healthcare data. **Data retention requirements** say how long data must be kept.
- **AUP (acceptable use policy):** the company's rules for using its equipment and network.
- **Regulatory and business compliance requirements:** often shown on **splash screens** at login.

## 4.7 Communication and professionalism

- **Appearance and attire:** match the environment. Formal or business casual.
- **Proper language:** avoid jargon, acronyms and slang where you can.
- **Positive attitude, project confidence.**
- **Actively listen, avoid interrupting.**
- **Be culturally sensitive:** use appropriate professional titles and designations.
- **Be on time.** If late, contact the customer.
- **Avoid distractions:** personal calls, texting and social media, personal interruptions.
- **Difficult customers:** do not argue or be defensive. Do not dismiss the issue. Do not be judgmental. **Clarify:** ask open-ended questions to narrow the scope, restate the issue, or ask a question to verify understanding. Use discretion and professionalism when discussing experiences.
- **Set and meet expectations and timelines, and communicate status.** Offer repair or replacement options as needed. Provide documentation of the services. **Follow up** later to verify satisfaction.
- **Confidential and private materials** on a computer, desktop, printer and so on: treat with care, don't read or share them.

## Common scenarios

| Scenario | Answer |
|---|---|
| About to replace a part in a PC that is plugged in | Disconnect the power first, then wear an ESD strap |
| Prohibited content found on a work computer | Stop, report to management, preserve evidence, chain of custody, document |
| Old laptop battery and toner cartridges to dispose of | Recycling or hazardous waste, using the MSDS guidance |
| Brief power dips are crashing PCs | UPS (and a surge suppressor for spikes) |
| Angry customer | Stay calm, listen, restate, never argue |
| Staff must agree to rules each login | AUP shown on a splash screen |
