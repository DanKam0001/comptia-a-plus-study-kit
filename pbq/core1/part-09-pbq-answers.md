## C1P09-PBQ1: Toolbox Match-up (8 points)

| # | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | D Crimper | A crimper attaches the RJ45 plug to the cable end |
| 2 | H Cable stripper | It removes the outer jacket without cutting the wires |
| 3 | B Punchdown tool | It seats the wires in the back of a patch panel and trims the excess |
| 4 | F Toner probe | A tone at one end, a probe traces the cable and beeps |
| 5 | A Cable tester | It checks continuity and miswires on every wire |
| 6 | G Loopback plug | It sends the signal back into a port to test the port itself |
| 7 | E Network tap | It copies traffic for inspection without interrupting the link |
| 8 | C Wi-Fi analyzer | It shows networks, channels and signal strength |

**Answer string:** 1D 2H 3B 4F 5A 6G 7E 8C

**Marking:** 1 point per correct match. No penalty for a wrong match, but each tool is used only once, so one swap costs two points.

## C1P09-PBQ2: New Branch Connection Form (10 points)

| Field | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Satellite | Reaches remote areas with no cable or phone line, and has high latency (delay) |
| A2 | WISP | A radio link from a local tower to a rooftop antenna that needs line of sight |
| A3 | Fiber | Glass cable and light, usually the fastest connection |
| A4 | ONT | The Optical Network Terminal is where fiber enters and turns light into Ethernet |
| B1 | 802.3af | 8 W is under the 15.4 W limit, and it is the lowest standard |
| B2 | 802.3at | 20 W is over 15.4 W but under the 30 W limit |
| B3 | 802.3bt Type 3 | 45 W is over 30 W but under the 60 W limit |
| B4 | 802.3bt Type 4 | 65 W is over 60 W but under the 90 W limit |
| C1 | PoE injector | A small box added between a normal switch and the device |
| C2 | PoE switch | Power sourcing is built into the switch, so no extra boxes |

**Marking:** 1 point per field. Part B: if you named a higher standard that would also work (for example 802.3bt Type 3 for B1), the rule asked for the lowest, so give 0 for that field.
