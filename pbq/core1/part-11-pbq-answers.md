## C1P11-PBQ1: Swap a Laptop Wi-Fi Card (7 points)

| Order | Step | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | D Shut down, unplug the charger, ESD strap | Safe replacement habits start before anything is opened |
| 2 | B Open the case with photos and labelled screws, disconnect the battery | Photos and labelled screw piles make reassembly easy, and the battery comes off before you touch parts |
| 3 | F Unsnap the two antenna leads and remove the old card | The old card has to come out before the new one goes in |
| 4 | A Fit the new card in the M.2 slot | The card must be seated before leads are attached to it |
| 5 | G Snap the two antenna leads on | Forgetting this gives a very weak signal |
| 6 | C Reconnect the battery and power on | Power returns only after the work is finished |
| 7 | E Test the Wi-Fi signal | You can only test once the laptop is powered on |

**Answer string:** D, B, F, A, G, C, E

**Marking:** 1 point for each step in its correct position.

## C1P11-PBQ2: Mobile Setup Form (11 points)

| Field | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | Docking station | One connection gives screen, keyboard, network and often charging, and can add ports. A port replicator only repeats the laptop's own ports |
| A2 | Tethering (hotspot) | One phone shares its internet with other devices |
| A3 | NFC | Very short range, about 4 cm, for tap to pay |
| A4 | MDM | Mobile Device Management enforces passcodes and remote wipe from one console |
| A5 | BYOD | Bring Your Own Device, often with a separate work profile |
| A6 | eSIM | Built into the phone and set up by QR code or profile |
| B1 | 24 | USB-C has 24 pins |
| B2 | Yes | USB-C is reversible |
| B3 | 8 | Lightning has 8 pins |
| B4 | 5 | miniUSB and microUSB have 5 pins |
| B5 | No | microUSB is not reversible |

Unused options: Lightning and Port replicator.

**Marking:** 1 point per field.
